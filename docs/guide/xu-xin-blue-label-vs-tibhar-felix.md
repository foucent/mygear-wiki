## 🔧 Construction comparison

| | Xu Xin Blue Label | Tibhar Felix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | **Outer** fiber | **Inner** fiber (W968-style) |
| Face ply | Stained **Koto** | **Limba** |
| Fiber | Stretched carbon — very thin, high strength | Super carbon |
| Force layer | Ayous | Ayous |
| Core | Ayous | Ayous |

## 🏓 Player evaluation keywords (by stage)

| Stage | Xu Xin Blue Label | Tibhar Felix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Short game** | Slightly springy; needs controlled touch; best with tacky rubbers | Stable, doesn't pop high; suits non-tacky / micro-tacky |
| **Close-table fast exchange** | More spin & power; trickier arc, higher threat — higher skill threshold | Easier & lighter; stable, continuous attack |
| **Mid-table rally** | Long oppressive arc, accelerating second bounce; bigger single-stroke threat | Great continuity, easy to keep looping |
| **Far-table rally** | High ceiling, deep bottom power; keeps rising as you swing | Runs out of steam; average bottom power |

---

## 💡 Key conclusions

- **Face wood sets the feel**: Koto = crisp & explosive; Limba = soft, grips the ball.
- Blue Label has deep bottom power (Ayous core + Stiga woodcraft); Felix is quick for an inner and fast to re-attack, but lacks depth.
- Felix is easier to play — stable and continuous. Blue Label has higher single-stroke threat but a higher skill threshold.
- Blue Label pairs best with Chinese tacky rubbers (**Hurricane 3**, **Skyline 2**); Felix pairs best with non-tacky or micro-tacky.
- Pick the **Blue Label** for aggressive first-three-shot play; pick the **Felix** for continuity and control.
